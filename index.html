Movement 11

  1. Acceleration and decelerationSpeed ramps up to max and back down to zero over a short time instead of snapping. The balance between the two decides whether movement feels tight or slippery.
  2. Air controlLet the player steer left and right during a jump, usually with weaker acceleration than on the ground.
  3. Turn-around speedGive turning around its own, stronger acceleration so reversing is quick even when stopping is gentle.
  4. Wall slideFalling while pushing into a wall slows the fall, giving the player time to line up a wall jump.
  5. Wall jump input lockoutRight after a wall jump, ignore the direction input for a moment so the jump actually carries the player away from the wall.
  6. Dash with cooldownA short burst of fixed speed in the facing direction, with a cooldown so it can't be chained into flying.
  7. Ledge grabWhen a jump ends with the player's head level with a ledge they are pushing into, they catch it and hang, then jump to climb up.
  8. Slope snappingWhen running downhill, keep the character on the slope instead of letting them skip down it in small hops.
  9. Moving platforms that carry the playerA player standing on a moving platform moves with it, and a jump off it keeps the platform's speed.
  10. Analog stick dead zoneIgnore tiny stick readings near the center, then rescale the rest so movement still starts smoothly from zero.
  11. Frame rate independenceRun the game's physics in fixed steps of real time, so it plays at the same speed on a 60 Hz laptop and a 144 Hz monitor.

Jumping 11

  1. Coyote timeFor a short moment after the player runs off a ledge, jump still works as if they were on the ground.
  2. Jump bufferingIf the player presses jump just before landing, remember the press for a short time and jump the moment they touch the ground.
  3. Variable jump heightTap jump for a short hop, hold it for a full jump. When the player lets go while still rising, cut the upward speed.
  4. Apex hangNear the top of a jump, while jump is held, gravity is cut so the player hangs for a moment and can line up the landing.
  5. Heavier fallGravity gets stronger once the character starts falling, so jumps rise smoothly and come down fast.
  6. Terminal velocityCap how fast the character can fall, so long drops stay readable and steerable.
  7. Corner correctionWhen a jump clips the corner of a ceiling by a few pixels, slide the player sideways around it instead of stopping the jump.
  8. Double jumpAllow one extra jump in the air, refilled when the player lands.
  9. Landing recoveryAfter a hard landing, the character can't act for a few frames, which gives falls weight.
  10. One-way platforms with drop-throughPlatforms you can jump up through from below and land on from above, with down plus jump to drop back through.
  11. Fast fallLet the player press down while falling to drop at a higher, fixed speed, so they decide when to come down fast.

Combat 11

  1. Input buffering for attacksA press made during an attack's recovery is saved and fires the moment the attack ends, instead of being thrown away.
  2. Invincibility frames with flickerAfter taking a hit, the player can't be hurt again for a short time, and the sprite flickers so they know.
  3. KnockbackA hit pushes the target away from the attacker with a small upward pop, then friction slows it down.
  4. Attack startup, active and recoverySplit each attack into a wind-up, a short window where it can hit, and a follow-through where the attacker is open.
  5. Cancel windowsAfter an attack connects, a short window lets the next attack start right away, skipping the rest of the recovery.
  6. Dodge rollA quick roll that can't be hurt for its length, so a well-timed roll passes straight through an attack.
  7. Aim assistWhen the player fires roughly toward a target, send the shot at it if it is inside a small cone and in range.
  8. Projectile leadingEnemies aim where the player will be when the shot arrives, not where they are now, so running in a straight line stops working.
  9. Attack telegraphEnemies show a clear wind-up before an attack becomes dangerous, so the player can react.
  10. Forgiving hitboxesMake hazard hitboxes a little smaller than their art, so grazing a spike tip doesn't count.
  11. RecoilPush the player back a little every time they fire, so each shot has some force behind it.

Camera 4

  1. Camera lookahead and smoothingThe camera aims a bit ahead of where the player is facing and eases toward that point instead of being glued to the player.
  2. Camera deadzoneThe camera stays still while the player moves inside a window in the middle of the screen, and only scrolls when they push past its edge.
  3. Camera platform snappingThe camera's height follows the player only when they land, so ordinary jumps don't bob the whole screen.
  4. Camera look-downHolding down while standing still pans the camera down after a short delay, so the player can check a drop before taking it.

Feedback 6

  1. HitstopFreeze the action for a few frames when a hit lands so the impact registers.
  2. Screen shake with falloffShake the camera on impact, with the strength fading over time instead of a constant jitter.
  3. Hit flashTurn the target fully white for a few frames when it gets hit, so the player sees the hit land.
  4. Damage numbersPop a number above the target on each hit showing the damage, then float it up and fade it out.
  5. Jump squash and stretchStretch the character tall on takeoff and squash them wide on landing, scaled by how hard they land, then ease back.
  6. Delayed damage barWhen health drops, a pale bar stays where the health was for a moment and then drains, showing how big the hit was.
