Running jump

Also called: speed-based jump height, momentum jump, run jump, jump higher when running

Make a jump a little stronger the faster the player is moving when they leave the ground, so a running start pays off.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Stand under the green ledge and jump. You can't reach it. Now take a run at it from the left and jump near the edge. With the boost on, the running jump makes it. Turn it off and both jumps go the same height.

What it does

With one fixed jump strength, a jump from a standstill and a jump at full sprint go exactly as high, and running stops mattering. Several Mario games scale the take-off by speed. Frame Advance's breakdown of Super Mario Bros. shows it picking one of five rows of jump values by horizontal speed, with a jump starting at 4 px per frame, or 5 when running. It lists Super Mario World with eight take-off speeds and Super Mario 64's single jump as 42 plus a quarter of forward speed. Players learn to take a run at big gaps, and levels can have a ledge that only a running jump reaches.

Use it when

Platformers with a run button or a wide range of speeds, where you want speed to matter. It works best with a few gaps or ledges that only a running jump can clear.

Watch out

If players can't tell why one jump reached the ledge and the next one didn't, it feels random. Keep the bonus modest, make full speed easy to see, and test every required jump at the speed players will really have when they get there. Scale by take-off speed only, not speed in the air.

Starting values

In Super Mario Bros. a running jump takes off 25% faster than a standing one (5 px per frame against 4), per Frame Advance. This demo adds up to 25% at full speed and scales it in between.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Make jump strength depend on running speed.
- When a jump starts, read the player's horizontal speed at take-off.
- Jump velocity = base jump velocity x (1 + 25% x take-off speed / max run speed). At full speed that is 25% stronger.
- Only take-off speed counts. Speeding up in the air doesn't change the jump.
- Keep variable jump height working: letting go of jump early still cuts the jump short.
- Make full speed easy to read (run animation, dust or a sound) so players can tell a running jump from a standing one.

Acceptance checks:
1. Log the height of a standing jump and a full-speed jump. The full-speed one is higher.
2. At full speed the take-off velocity is 25% higher than standing.
3. Every jump the level requires can be made at the speed a player can reach before it.
