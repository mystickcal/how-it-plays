Terminal velocity

Also called: max fall speed, fall speed cap, fall cap

Cap how fast the character can fall, so long drops stay readable and steerable.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Walk off the ledge into the shaft (it loops) and steer around the red balls. Turn the cap off and watch the fall speed climb.

What it does

Gravity adds speed every frame. Without a cap, a long fall gets faster and faster until the player can't react to anything on the way down, and fast bodies can skip through thin obstacles. A max fall speed clamps vertical speed after gravity is added. Some games also let the player hold down to fall faster, up to a second, higher cap.

Use it when

Any game with gravity and falls longer than one jump.

Watch out

A cap that is too low makes falling feel like gliding. Check collisions at the capped speed so nothing tunnels.

Starting values

Celeste's public player code caps falling at 160, or 240 while holding down, in pixels per second at 320 by 180. That is 320 and 480 px/s at this demo's size.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Cap the player's fall speed.
- After adding gravity each frame, clamp vertical speed to at most 320 px/s downward.
- Optional: while down is held, raise the cap gradually to about 1.5 times that.
- Upward speed is not capped by this.

Acceptance checks:
1. A long fall never goes faster than 320 px/s. Log the speed.
2. Short jumps feel the same as before.
3. At the capped speed the player never passes through a thin platform.
