Aim assist

Also called: auto-aim, bullet magnetism, aim correction, target snap

When the player fires roughly toward a target, send the shot at it if it is inside a small cone and in range.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump · X or J shoot

Try this: Shoot the floating targets with X. The faint wedge shows the cone. Turn assist off and you only hit what is level with you.

What it does

Aiming precisely with keys, a stick or a touch screen is hard, so many games quietly correct the shot. At fire time, look for targets inside a cone around the aim direction and within range, pick the one closest by angle, and fire at it. Halo calls this auto-aim or bullet magnetism. Doom's public source aims bullets this way: it looks for a target straight ahead, then a few degrees to each side, and sets the shot's vertical angle to hit it.

Use it when

Shooters with gamepad or touch input, and any game where the player can't aim freely, like this one, which only shoots left or right.

Watch out

Too wide a cone feels like the game plays itself and can pick the wrong target when several are close. Competitive players notice it.

Starting values

Doom checks about 5.6 degrees to each side (1<<26 in its angle units) before giving up. A cone of 5 to 15 degrees is a sensible starting range.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add aim assist to shooting.
- When the player fires, find targets within 420 px whose direction is within 10 degrees of the aim direction.
- If there are any, fire at the one with the smallest angle difference. Otherwise fire straight.
- Only change the new shot's direction. Never move the player's aim or the camera.
- Make the cone and range settings, with a way to turn it off.

Acceptance checks:
1. Firing at a target slightly above or below the aim line hits it.
2. A target more than 10 degrees off is not hit unless aimed at.
3. With assist off, shots go exactly where aimed.
