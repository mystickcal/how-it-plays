Recoil

Also called: player knockback when shooting, gun kick, shot push back, weapon recoil

Push the player back a little every time they fire, so each shot has some force behind it.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump · X or J shoot

Try this: Tap X to shoot and watch the player slide back. Tap it fast and the pushes add up. Turn recoil off and the same shots feel lighter.

What it does

A shot that leaves the gun while nothing happens to the shooter feels like a toy. A short push opposite the aim direction on each shot makes the weapon feel strong, and with rapid fire the pushes add up, so firing nonstop has a cost. In his talk "The art of screenshake", Jan Willem Nijman of Vlambeer adds player knockback while shooting as one of the steps that turn a flat shooter into a punchy one, alongside screen shake and camera kick.

Use it when

Shooters, and any weapon that should feel heavy. Make it bigger for big guns and tiny or zero for fast, light ones.

Watch out

Too much push throws off aim and can shove players into hazards or off ledges. Apply it as a velocity that wears off, not a jump in position, and keep it small where the platforming needs precision.

Starting values

No checked source gives a number. Start with a push of 50 to 150 px/s per shot that wears off within about a tenth of a second, then tune it per weapon.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add recoil to shooting.
- When the player fires, add 120 px/s of horizontal velocity opposite the aim direction.
- Let that extra velocity wear off at 1500 px/s² until it reaches zero, separate from normal movement.
- Stack the pushes from rapid fire, but cap the total so the player can't be launched.
- Run the push through normal collision so it never moves the player into a wall or hazard.
- Make the push size a per-weapon setting, with a way to turn it off.

Acceptance checks:
1. One shot moves the player back a few pixels, then they stop.
2. Firing five times quickly moves them back further, up to the cap.
3. Recoil never pushes the player through a wall.
4. With recoil off, shooting does not move the player.
