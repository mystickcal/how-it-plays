Forgiving hitboxes

Also called: generous hitboxes, small hazard hitbox, hurtbox shrink, damage forgiveness

Make hazard hitboxes a little smaller than their art, so grazing a spike tip doesn't count.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Jump over the spikes and clip the tips. The outline shows the box that actually hurts.

What it does

When the hitbox matches the art exactly, players get hurt by hits they are sure missed, especially on pointy shapes like spikes. Shrinking the damage box a few pixels inside the art, and the player's hurtbox a little inside their sprite, removes those near misses. Players rarely notice the gap, but they always notice getting hurt by one pixel.

Use it when

Hazards, enemy bodies, bullets: anything that hurts the player.

Watch out

Don't shrink the player's own attacks against enemies. There, a slightly larger hitbox is the forgiving choice.

Starting values

Game Maker's Toolkit notes that Celeste's spikes have a small hitbox, so touching the very tip doesn't kill you. Rodrigo Monteiro's guide says it's better for the player to dodge a hit they should have taken than to take one they shouldn't. 3 to 6 px of shrink at this size is a starting point.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Make damage hitboxes forgiving.
- Shrink each hazard's damage box by 5 px on each side compared to its art, a little more on the pointy top edge of spikes.
- Shrink the player's hurtbox about 2 px inside the sprite.
- Leave solid collision boxes unchanged. This only changes what hurts.
- Add a debug view that draws the damage boxes.

Acceptance checks:
1. Grazing a spike tip does no damage.
2. Landing squarely on spikes still hurts.
3. The player stands on solid ground exactly as before.
