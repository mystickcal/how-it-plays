Camera platform snapping

Also called: platform snap, vertical camera snap, landing camera, ground-locked camera

The camera's height follows the player only when they land, so ordinary jumps don't bob the whole screen.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Climb the steps, then jump in place on the top one. Compare how much the camera moves up and down with snapping on and off.

What it does

A camera that follows the player vertically moves the whole world up and down on every jump. Platform snapping holds the vertical position while the player is in the air and eases to the new height after they land. Margins at the top and bottom still pull the camera if the player goes far enough. Itay Keren's camera talk credits Super Mario World with this.

Use it when

Platformers with a lot of jumping and vertical levels.

Watch out

Long falls and very high jumps still need the top and bottom margins, or the player leaves the screen.

Starting values

No checked source gives numbers. Ease to the landing height with a speed of about 4 to 8 (exponential smoothing), and keep margins of about 40 px at the top and bottom of the screen.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Make the camera snap vertically to platforms.
- Keep a target camera height. Update it only while the player is on the ground: target = player's feet - 62% of the screen height.
- In the air, keep the target, unless the player gets within 40 px of the top or bottom of the screen. Then push the target to keep them on screen.
- Each frame: camera.y += (target - camera.y) * (1 - Math.exp(-6 * dt)).

Acceptance checks:
1. Jumping in place doesn't move the camera vertically.
2. Landing on a higher platform eases the camera up within about half a second.
3. A long fall still keeps the player on screen.
