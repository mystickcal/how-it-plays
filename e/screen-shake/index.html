Screen shake with falloff

Also called: camera shake, trauma shake, impact shake

Shake the camera on impact, with the strength fading over time instead of a constant jitter.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump · X or J attack

Try this: Hit the dummy with X a few times in a row. If your system asks for reduced motion, this demo starts with shake off.

What it does

Each impact adds trauma, a number from 0 to 1. Shake strength is trauma squared, so small hits barely move the view and big ones hit hard, and trauma falls back to 0 at a steady rate. That approach comes from Squirrel Eiserloh's GDC talk. Use smooth noise rather than a new random offset each frame, shake only the world camera and never the UI, and give players a way to turn it off.

Use it when

Explosions, heavy hits, landing from a big fall.

Watch out

Every small event. Constant shake is tiring, and some players get motion sick.

Starting values

Max offset 15 to 25 px at this 640 px view (one hit at 0.5 trauma moves about a quarter of that), falloff 1.5 to 2.5 trauma per second.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add screen shake with falloff using a trauma value.
- trauma is 0 to 1. Each hit adds 0.5 (clamped to 1). trauma decreases by 1.8 per second.
- Each frame: shake = 20 px * trauma * trauma. Offset the world camera by shake times smooth noise (sum of two sine waves or Perlin noise), not a new random number per frame.
- Apply it only to the world camera. HUD and menus never shake.
- Add a settings toggle to turn shake off, and default it to off when prefers-reduced-motion is set.

Acceptance checks:
1. One hit gives a visible shake that settles within about 0.3 second(s).
2. Several quick hits shake harder but never past 20 px.
3. HUD text stays still while the world shakes.
4. With the setting off, nothing moves.
