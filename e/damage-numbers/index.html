Damage numbers

Also called: floating damage text, damage popups, hit numbers, floating combat text

Pop a number above the target on each hit showing the damage, then float it up and fade it out.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump · X or J attack

Try this: Hit the dummy on the ground and in the air. Jump attacks show bigger gold numbers.

What it does

Numbers tell the player how strong a hit was, which matters as soon as damage varies (critical hits, combos, upgrades). Spawn a small text at the hit point, move it up while slowing it down, and fade it near the end of its life. Offset each new number a little so quick hits don't stack on one spot, and make big hits bigger or a different color.

Use it when

RPGs, roguelikes, anything with damage that varies.

Watch out

Games where a clean screen matters more, or where damage never changes. Too many numbers turn into noise.

Starting values

No checked source gives timings. 500 to 900 ms of life with a quick rise that slows down is a starting point.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add floating damage numbers.
- On each hit, spawn a text with the damage at the top of the target, offset a few pixels sideways from the previous one.
- It rises at 90 px/s, slowing as it ages, and lives 700 ms. Fade it out over the last 200 ms.
- Bigger or critical hits use a larger font and a different color.
- Draw a dark outline or shadow so it reads on any background.

Acceptance checks:
1. Every hit shows its number once.
2. Quick hits don't stack numbers on the same pixel.
3. Numbers disappear after 700 ms.
