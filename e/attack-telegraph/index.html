Attack telegraph

Also called: wind-up tell, attack warning, anticipation, tell

Enemies show a clear wind-up before an attack becomes dangerous, so the player can react.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: The block on the right slams and spikes cover the floor. Jump when you see it wind up. With the telegraph off, slams come with no warning.

What it does

An attack that is dangerous on the frame it starts can't be avoided by reacting, only by luck. A telegraph adds a visible wind-up (a pose, a color change, a marked danger zone) for a fixed time before the attack turns on. The wind-up has to be longer than reaction time plus the time the dodge needs.

Use it when

Every enemy attack the player is meant to dodge or block.

Watch out

Keep tells consistent: the same wind-up always means the same attack. Very long tells make fights slow.

Starting values

No checked source gives one number. 300 to 600 ms of wind-up is a starting range; it has to cover reaction time plus the dodge.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add telegraphs to enemy attacks.
- Before an attack becomes active, play a 450 ms wind-up: change the enemy's color or pose and mark the danger area.
- The attack can't deal damage during the wind-up.
- Use the same tell every time for the same attack.
- Randomize when attacks start, not how long the tell lasts.

Acceptance checks:
1. Every attack comes after a visible 450 ms wind-up.
2. A player who reacts to the tell can always avoid the attack.
3. No damage happens during the wind-up.
