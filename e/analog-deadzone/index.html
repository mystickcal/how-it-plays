Analog stick dead zone

Also called: thumbstick dead zone, stick drift fix, scaled radial dead zone, input dead zone

Ignore tiny stick readings near the center, then rescale the rest so movement still starts smoothly from zero.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Let go of all keys and watch the character with the dead zone off. The circle at the top left shows the simulated stick.

What it does

Analog sticks rarely rest at exactly zero. Worn or loose sticks report a small value when nobody touches them, and the character creeps. A dead zone treats readings under a threshold as zero. Rescaling the rest of the range back to 0 to 1 avoids a jump in speed at the edge of the dead zone. This demo simulates a drifting stick with the keyboard: the arrow keys push a virtual stick that has some drift built in.

Use it when

Any game that reads analog sticks or triggers.

Watch out

Too large a dead zone makes small, precise moves impossible. Test with a real controller.

Starting values

Josh Sutphin's article on dead zones puts the usual threshold between 0.1 and 0.2 and recommends a scaled radial dead zone for 2D stick input.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add a dead zone to analog stick input.
- Read the stick as a 2D vector. If its length is under 0.2, treat it as zero.
- Otherwise rescale: direction * (length - 0.2) / (1 - 0.2), so output starts at 0 at the dead zone edge and reaches 1 at full tilt.
- Apply it to the vector, not to each axis separately, so diagonals stay smooth.
- Make the threshold a setting.

Acceptance checks:
1. A resting stick with drift under the threshold moves nothing.
2. Pushing just past the dead zone gives a small speed, not a jump.
3. Full tilt still reaches full speed.
