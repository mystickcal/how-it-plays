Camera deadzone

Also called: camera window, camera trap, dead zone camera, follow window

The camera stays still while the player moves inside a window in the middle of the screen, and only scrolls when they push past its edge.

←→ or AD move · Space W ↑ or Z jump

Try this: Take small steps left and right. With the window on, the camera stays put. Then run, and it follows.

What it does

A camera locked to the player moves every time they take a step, including small back-and-forth adjustments, and that constant motion is tiring to watch. A camera window lets the player move freely inside a box. When they push against an edge, the camera moves just enough to keep them inside. Itay Keren's camera talk traces this back to early arcade games and calls it a camera-window.

Use it when

Side-scrollers where the player makes lots of small moves.

Watch out

A wide window shows less of what's ahead while running. Combine it with lookahead or keep it narrow.

Starting values

No checked source gives a size. A half-width of 40 to 100 px at this 640 px view is a starting point.

Prompt for your coding agent

The numbers follow the sliders above. Works with plain canvas, Phaser, Three.js or Godot.

Add a camera window (deadzone) to horizontal follow.
- The window spans 80 px to each side of the screen center.
- If the player's center goes past the window's right edge, move the camera right by the overshoot. Same on the left.
- Inside the window the camera doesn't move.
- Clamp to the level bounds afterwards.

Acceptance checks:
1. Stepping back and forth within 80 px of center never moves the camera.
2. Running pushes the camera so the player stays at the window edge.
3. No jitter when the player stops at the edge.
